# DefCoreLib v0.5.0

The first release since 0.3.2, and it covers two development cycles at once — 0.4.0 was prepared but never published, so everything below is new to you.

Two new frame blocks join the Casing, a Ratchet valve arrives, and mechanisms learn to carry glued machines as one rigid body. On top of that, five new Mechanism blocks turn the rotation network into ship propulsion — three tiers of Propeller, a fuel-burning Thruster, and a Reaction wheel — and the library grows the API a dependent plugin needs to drive them. Also: an in-game item catalog, a pick-block change, and a fix for a rotation-network server hang under redstone pulse storms.

## Read before upgrading

- **Built against Paper 1.21.11** (was 1.21.8). Mechanism additionally needs the datapack-discovery bootstrap API, so run 1.21.11 or newer. `api-version` is still `1.21`, so an older server will load the plugin and fail at runtime rather than refuse it cleanly at startup.
- **Bare shafts change material.** A shaft wrenched to its bare form was an iron chain; it is now a **waxed copper chain**. Existing worlds are converted in place as their chunks load, keeping each shaft's axis — no action needed, but the block in your world will not be the one you left there.
- **Carried windmills supply half power.** A windmill supplies its full rating on a static build, but riding a mechanism it now gives 1 / 3 / 8 instead of 1 / 5 / 15. Rotation power is all-or-nothing, so a contraption running near break-even off its own windmills will stop entirely until it gains a windmill or sheds a consumer.
- **Mixed-wood casing frames no longer auto-glue.** Frame blocks bond by matching wood now. Rebuild a frame in a single wood, or brush-pin the mixed joints.

## Highlights

- **Gearbox** and **Chassis** — two new mechanical frame blocks, in all 12 woods
- **Frame rework** — casings, gearboxes, and chassis auto-glue by matching wood, and can be brush-pinned rigidly to anything
- **Carried contraptions** — mechanisms now move glued machines (a chain hoist rides a rotator, load and all)
- **Ceiling rotator** — a rotator can mount under a block as a swinging hinge
- **Ceiling propellers and windmills** — all three tiers of each now mount under a block too, on a shaft running up into it; functionally identical to a floor mount
- **Ratchet** — a new one-way rotation valve
- **Ship propulsion** — Propeller (3 tiers), Thruster, Reaction wheel; see the mech changelog
- **Bigger glued selections** — the cap on one glued assembly rose from 256 blocks to 1024
- **Item catalog** — browse every registered block in-game with `/defcorelib catalog`
- **Stability** — fixes a server-thread hang under comparator/observer pulse storms; steadier mover speeds

## Blocks

- **Gearbox** — an omnidirectional power hub: it passes power in every direction, on all six faces, but not spin direction — each side settles its own rotation. Split one input into several outputs, or turn a corner without a gear's counter-rotation.
- **Chassis** — a slime-reach casing: it grabs every adjacent movable block except honey, so a moving contraption drags its whole payload with no glue brush, while still joining a same-wood casing frame.
- **Ratchet** — a one-way valve for rotation: it drives one way and freewheels (disconnects) the other, instead of jamming. Wrench to set the direction, sneak-wrench to inspect.
- **Propeller / Thruster / Reaction wheel** — consumers that push a ship rather than the world. They draw power and produce none, and have no effect on their own; a BlockShips vessel reads their powered state and decides what the thrust does.

## Frame glue

- Casing, gearbox, and chassis are one **frame** family keyed by wood — they auto-glue only to same-wood frame blocks. The glue brush pins one rigidly to anything (any wood, or a plain block).

## Contraptions

- Movers (piston, rotator, drawbridge, minecart, chain hoist) now carry **nested glued machines** — a chain hoist glued onto a rotator swings as one body, head + chain + platform together.
- Rotators can mount under a block as a **ceiling hinge**, swinging the block above like a hatch.
- **Cushions, item frames and paintings ride along.** Anything resting on or hanging from an assembled block used to be left behind and pop off as an item. Now a 26.3 **cushion** rides the move with whoever sits on it and lands back on its block with them still seated, and **item frames** (with their item) and **paintings** travel with the structure and re-hang on the landed block, turned with it. If the landing has nowhere for them to go, they drop as items rather than vanish. A painting can't hang sideways, so a drawbridge that rolls one onto its side drops it. Cushion support switches itself off on servers older than 26.3. (mech plugin)

## Items & commands

- **In-game item catalog** — `/defcorelib catalog` opens a browsable menu of every registered block across all def plugins, grouped by category, with search (`/defcorelib catalog search <query>`, or `/defcorelib catalog <category>` to jump straight in) and a detail view that drills into each recipe's ingredients — recipe sections with many outputs open a paginated, fully browsable view. Open to everyone via the new `corelib.catalog` permission (default on); an admin can right-click an entry to give the item.
- **Pick-block** — middle-clicking a custom block or cart no longer mints a free copy in survival or adventure: it only selects a matching item you already carry. Creative still gives the item as before.

## API

- `isRotationPowered(int)`, `rotationSurplus(int)`, `rotationFacing(int)`, `rotationFacingAt(Block)` — live rotation state. `powered`, `surplus` and `jammed` are **per-network**, identical for every member; do not build a per-block model on them.
- `MechanismRotationSolvedEvent` — fires when a mechanism's rotation network re-solves. Topology is frozen while assembled and the solver runs at most every 20 ticks, so this is cheap; a windmill-only mechanism never re-solves after assembly.
- `bannerTiersIn(World, BoundingBox)` — large/huge banner displays by host block. Three things to get right: **expand the region by at least 3 blocks**, because a banner's display entity spawns in the neighbour cell toward the face it hangs on and scales out from there, so a box fitted tightly to a structure misses precisely the banners mounted on its outside; **only chunks whose ENTITIES are loaded are searched** — Paper streams entities in after blocks, so a query made in that window sees the host blocks and none of the banners, and a caller who cannot tolerate a wrong answer should gate on `Chunk.isEntitiesLoaded()` and treat "not yet" as unknown rather than zero; and `BannerTier.NORMAL` entries are **fence flags and bed banners, never banner blocks** — a banner placed as a block creates no display at all, so a material-based count and this API cannot double up.
- `massRegistry()` — exposes the mass table so a dependent plugin can price blocks its own config does not list.
- **`requires_plugin` recipe gate** — withhold a block's recipes until a named plugin enables, then register them live.
- `CushionSeats` (experimental) — 26.3 cushions for a consumer that seats players itself: find the cushions resting on a set of blocks, and the carried cushion riding a seat shulker. Safe on every version: before 26.3 nothing is a cushion and every answer is empty.

## Behavior

- **The BetterBanners tier gate is a recipe gate and nothing more.** Without that plugin the large and huge **Windmills** are uncraftable, but tier blocks that already exist keep placing, dropping, capturing onto mechanisms, and supplying their full tier power. The gate stops at the windmill: converting a tier Windmill into a tier Propeller is ungated, so one obtained from creative or from a world that used to run BetterBanners still converts tier-for-tier instead of being silently downgraded. The startup log line said "unavailable", which read as though those blocks stopped working; it now says "uncraftable; existing ones still work".

## Fixes

- **Land-claim protection now applies to custom carts and banners.** Breaking a custom cart inside a claim a protection plugin refused still removed it and dropped its item and contents; and placing or shearing fence flags, large, huge and bed banners skipped protection plugins entirely, since they are display entities rather than blocks. Both now respect claims.
- **The glue brush respects land claims.** Gluing changes no block, so protection plugins never saw it: a player could glue someone else's wall to their own rotator, door or minecart and carry it off, or open and clear the glue on a door inside someone else's claim. Each glued block, and the anchor itself, is now checked with your protection plugin first. Glue made before a claim existed, and blocks pulled along by slime, honey or a chassis, are not re-checked.
- **Ships survive a crash right after docking.** A persisted contraption (a BlockShips ship) deleted its save the moment it was taken apart, before the world had saved the placed blocks — a crash in that window lost the whole ship and its cargo. The save is now kept until the world has written the blocks (including while saving is paused with `/save-off`). Persisted contraptions are also saved at every world save, so after a crash their cargo matches players' inventories instead of duplicating or losing what was moved in between.
- **Ship cargo dupe** — a cargo menu left open while the ship's chunk unloaded let a player take items that came back when the ship reloaded. The menu now closes.
- **Unreadable cargo is kept** — one item a ship's save couldn't read (say, after removing a datapack) emptied that container permanently on the next save. The saved contents are now carried over untouched.
- **Custom blocks placed shortly before a crash are recognised again** — a container-based custom block (water wheel, boiler, mechanical dispenser) could come back as a plain vanilla block: wrong drop, sealed slots open, machine dead.
- **Refused placements leave nothing behind** — a custom block whose placement a protection plugin refused left an invisible marker that turned the next matching vanilla block placed there into the custom one.
- **Gluing to a BlockShips ship wheel** now asks your protection plugin first, like every other glue anchor.
- **Glue can't be pointed at new blocks** — a chain hoist's glue on blocks that are gone is dropped instead of grabbing whatever is at the new depth, and a glued mechanism cart pushed off its cell loses its glue instead of assembling the blocks around its new spot.
- **Box-gluing is bounded** — only the blocks actually being glued are checked against claims (a big box used to fire tens of thousands of checks in one click), and a box must be under 128 blocks across with every chunk loaded.
- A rotator whose landing hit an error no longer stays stuck "moving" forever.
- Chunk unloads and hopper transfers beside custom blocks cost less, and the world-save snapshot of persisted contraptions is written in the background and skipped when nothing changed.
- **Server hang under pulse storms** — the rotation network and custom rails now coalesce their recomputes to once per tick, and the plugin's own block writes no longer feed their own physics loop.
- **Wall heads on mechanisms no longer render backwards** — the head item model's face is a half-turn from the block's, and the mechanism display path now compensates.
- **Contraptions reliably survive a crash** — a parked mechanism is rediscovered from its own entities when its chunk loads, so a hard crash can no longer strand an invisible, unusable contraption.
- Mover speeds slowed to steadier, more readable rates.
- **Head-yaw conventions documented.** Four sites in this library orient a head on a display entity (five counting BlockShips', six counting the standing-banner transform that shares the same maths), and they fall into two families differing by a constant half-turn. The javadoc claimed there were three conventions and that the redstone dynamo shared the mechanism spawn path; both were wrong. The remaining open question — whether floor heads need the same half-turn wall heads got — is stated with the one two-block test that settles it, and deliberately left unchanged, since altering it would rotate every floor head on every mechanism in every existing world.

See the per-plugin **mech** notes for full detail, recipes, and advancements.
